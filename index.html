01 Software engineering Loopsio · 2026

ALICS: productionising computer vision for structural inspection

Role Software developer, team of 4 Duration 16 weeks Evidence film below

Problem. Three research computer-vision models existed only as lab code: YOLO detection, U-Net segmentation and photometric-stereo 3D reconstruction. Civil engineers needed to run all three offline and on site, without installing Python or working in a terminal.

Approach. I built the application layer around the existing analysis code: a Tauri 2 and Rust backend, a React 19 and TypeScript frontend, and a zero-copy Rust-to-Python inference bridge over PyO3 that uses NumPy ndarray views, so that multi-megapixel images cross the language boundary without serialisation. Results are persisted per scan and per cell in a SQLite layer with twelve migrations, one of which moved image data into BLOB storage to resolve row-size limits on large scans.

Outcome. The tool ships as signed installers for Windows, macOS and Linux with the Python runtime bundled, so a surveyor installs it like any other desktop application. It reports crack width in millimetres against a calibration target, renders a Three.js mesh with a signed depth scale, and exports templated PDF inspection reports suitable for structural documentation.

02 Operations research Coursework, Mathematical Programming in Advanced Analytics · 2026

School connectivity optimisation under budget uncertainty

Role Geospatial pipeline and modelling, team project Solver Gurobi via Julia/JuMP Evidence dashboard below

Problem. Around 93% of Honduran public schools have no internet connection. Connecting one requires a three-layer prerequisite chain that has to be built in order: a tower, then WISP equipment, then devices in the school itself. Building a new tower costs eighteen times as much as equipping an existing one, and the national budget is both phased over five years and uncertain at the outset.

Approach. The team formulated a multi-period mixed-integer linear programme: budgeted maximal covering, prerequisite sequencing constraints, soft annual deployment milestones, and a two-stage stochastic extension hedging a ±20% budget shock in years 3 to 5. I built the geospatial pipeline over 15,000+ schools and 7,297 candidate tower sites, using a 10 km Haversine coverage radius.

Outcome. Left unrestricted, the model connects 98.7% of in-scope schools. Imposing the political deployment milestones brings that down to 74.4%, a gap of 3,046 schools, or roughly 125 schools for every percentage point of early visibility bought. We solved both versions deliberately, so that the cost of the milestones could be quoted as a figure rather than argued about, and both are embedded in the dashboard below. The stochastic extension secures a 1,067-school core that survives a budget cut, with EVPI and VSS bounds of at least 263 and 177 schools respectively.

03 Machine learning & statistics Personal project · 2026

World Cup predictor, validated tournament by tournament

Role Solo, data pipeline to validation Validation Leave-one-tournament-out CV · 100k Monte Carlo

Problem. Sports prediction is unusually prone to leakage. Features are easily built on information that did not exist at the time of the match, and held-out tournaments bleed into training through shared history. The pipeline is designed around preventing both.

Approach. Regex state machines parse 1,402 matches from every World Cup since 1930, plus Euro and Copa América. A sequential FeatureBuilder enforces read-then-update, so features for match i use only matches 0 to i−1. 42 features cover Elo with confederation-tuned K-factors, career goal statistics, recent form, tournament experience and head-to-head record. A LightGBM classifier is blended 75/25 with a Poisson expected-goals GLM, tuned over 300 Optuna trials.

Outcome. The model beats the majority-class baseline in all nine leave-one-tournament-out folds, 1990 to 2022, averaging 52.4% against 45.0%. Its best fold is 2006, at 64.1%; its worst is 2002, at 43.8%, where the baseline was also at its weakest. Tournament experience carries more weight than raw Elo. Confederation membership carries none once performance features are in.

2026. The bracket simulation was run before the tournament and put no team above 15.3%, which is to say it treated the field as open rather than backing a favourite. Spain went on to win. The model placed Spain fourth, at 9.2% to win the tournament and 16.0% to reach the final.
